Mountain Fronts in VR

A sketch of how Smf classes could link the surface to the faults beneath it

Each block is one mountain front, coloured by its Smf class. The cut face at the front shows the thrust fault underneath. Look at the shape of each front, then go underground to see why it looks that way.

Drag to move around, scroll to zoom

Class 1 / Active

Smf ≤ 1.65

The fault reaches the surface and keeps pushing the range up faster than rivers can cut into it, so the front stays straight.

Class 2 / Slowing

Smf 1.66 – 2.5

The fault still moves, but slowly. Rivers start to catch up and carve shallow bays into the front.

Class 3 / Inactive

Smf > 2.5

The fault has stopped and is buried under sediment shed off the range. Erosion has worn the front back into deep bays.
